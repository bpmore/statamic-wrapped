# Brief: soundtracks for Wrapped

Paste everything below the line into a Claude conversation. It produces the
Suno prompts for the addon's bundled music. Keep this file with the repo so
the brief is versioned alongside the tracks it produced.

---

You are writing music prompts for Suno. Read all of this before writing anything.

## What the music is for

Wrapped is a free addon for Statamic, a content management system used by teams all over the world. Once a year it turns a site's publishing history into a short vertical video for social media: one fact per screen ("You published 47 entries", "You publish most on Tuesday afternoons", "This page has quietly done its job since 2021"), with a music track underneath. The tone is warm and celebratory. It is a small pat on the back for a content team, never a hype reel.

The video is 30 to 45 seconds long, vertical, and will be posted to places like LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok by people who run websites: agencies, universities, small companies, solo bloggers.

The addon ships with a set of tracks and the person making the video picks one. The pick is a matter of taste, so the set should feel like a real choice: different moods, different parts of the world, different energies.

## What I need from you

Twelve tracks. For each one, give me exactly this:

1. **Name** (two or three words, for a dropdown menu; a mood or a place, never a genre label)
2. **One-line description** (under twelve words, shown beside the name so someone can choose without listening)
3. **Suno style prompt** (the text pasted into Suno's style field; see the rules below)
4. **Tempo** (a BPM range)
5. **Why it fits Wrapped** (one sentence; I will not show this to users, it is for me)

## Rules for the Suno prompts

- **Instrumental only.** Say "instrumental" and "no vocals" in every prompt. Words on the soundtrack would fight the words on screen, and the videos are made in many languages.
- **Warm, forward-moving, unhurried.** Celebratory but not triumphant. Think "a good year, quietly" rather than "champions." Nothing tense, nothing melancholy, nothing that sounds like a car advert.
- **Clean, simple arrangement.** The music sits under text. Sparse beats fuller. No sudden drops, no big builds, no dramatic silences. A steady groove with a gentle lift in the last third is ideal.
- **A natural ending.** Prompt for a track that resolves rather than fades or loops. Ask for roughly 45 seconds if Suno allows a length hint.
- **Describe sound, not people.** Never name a real artist, band, song, or label. Suno refuses those and it is not what I want anyway. Describe instruments, rhythm, texture, mood, era.
- **Original only.** I will not use Suno's remix feature. Each prompt must stand alone.

## Rules for the set as a whole

- **From all over the world, and respectful about it.** Statamic runs on every continent. I want the set to sound like it knows that: West Africa, Brazil, Japan, South Asia, the Middle East, the Caribbean, Scandinavia, Ireland, the Andes, Korea, the Balkans, the Mediterranean, and so on. Pick twelve regions that give real variety.
- **No caricature.** Each track should sound like something a musician from that place would actually make today, not a tourist postcard. Modern production is fine and often better: a contemporary producer working in a tradition, not a museum recording. If a tradition would be trivialised by a 45-second instrumental with beats under it, choose a different one.
- **Avoid anything sacred, ceremonial, or national.** No hymns, no anthems, no music tied to religious ritual or state occasions. This plays under "you uploaded 12 files."
- **Spread the moods.** Across the twelve I want roughly: four gentle and reflective, four bright and upbeat, four somewhere in between. Nobody's Wrapped should have to be a party if their year was calm.
- **Include two or three that are not tied to a place** (a lo-fi beat, a soft piano and strings piece, a light electronic groove). Some people just want neutral.

## Format

Number them 1 to 12. Plain text, no tables. Put the Suno prompt on its own line in quotation marks so I can copy it cleanly. After the twelve, add a short note on anything you deliberately left out and why.
